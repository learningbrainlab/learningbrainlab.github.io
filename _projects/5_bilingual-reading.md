**Investigating Bilingual Reading Networks**

**Period:** 2016 – 2023  
**Funding:**
- *Effective Biliteracy: The Impact of Script Sets on Bilingual Reading Networks for Typical and Atypical Readers*, CRADLE–Johns Hopkins University Collaborative Grant (2016 – 2021; led the neuroimaging studies)
- *How Language Mixes Contribute to Effective Bilingualism and Biliteracy in Singapore*, National Research Foundation, Science of Learning Initiative (2017 – 2023; Co-PI)

#### Overview

These projects examine the neurocognitive mechanisms behind typical and impaired reading in bilinguals. Combining behavioural assessment with neuroimaging, the studies follow bilingual readers from early childhood to adulthood and investigate how factors such as the orthographic depth of a language and auditory perception skills shape the brain's reading network.

#### Why it matters

Most of what we know about the reading brain comes from monolingual readers. Understanding how bilingual readers' brains adapt to two writing systems helps inform neuroscience-based approaches to supporting bilingual children with reading difficulties.
